How to measure your bathroom for a vanity

A vanity has to fit the wall, clear the plumbing, and leave room for the door and toilet. Here's how to capture all of that in a few measurements so we can build a free 3D design and an exact quote.

  1. Measure the vanity wall

    Measure the full width of the wall the vanity will sit against, in inches, from corner to corner or from the wall to the edge of the doorway/shower. This is the hard limit your vanity has to fit inside. Note it on a quick sketch.

  2. Check the depth you can give up

    Measure how far the vanity can project into the room without blocking the door swing, shower or toilet clearance. Standard vanities are about 21″ deep; floating and shallow builds can go less. Make sure a person can still stand comfortably in front.

  3. Locate the plumbing

    Mark the height and horizontal position of the drain and water supply lines coming out of the wall or floor, measured from the corner and from the floor. This decides whether a standard cabinet clears the plumbing or needs a notch, and where the sink can sit.

  4. Mark the doorway, toilet and obstructions

    Note the distance from the vanity wall to the toilet, the door and its swing, and any window, radiator or light switch. Leave at least a few inches between the vanity edge and the toilet so both are usable.

  5. Decide single vs double

    A single-sink vanity suits widths up to about 48″; plan a double-sink run from roughly 60″ of clear wall. If you want a floating (wall-mounted) vanity, also note where the studs are for the mounting cleat.

  6. Send it for a free 3D design

    Photograph your sketch with the wall width, depth, plumbing position and clearances, and send it over. We return a free 3D layout and an itemized, no-obligation quote - plus free finish samples to confirm the look before you order.

Standard vanity sizes to plan around: ~36″ W × 21″ D × 34½″ H; single up to ~48″ W; double from ~60″ W.

Bathroom measuring FAQs

What are standard bathroom vanity dimensions?

The standard footprint is about 36″ wide × 21″ deep × 34½″ high. Single-sink vanities run up to about 48″ wide; double-sink vanities typically start around 60″ and go to 72″. Floating vanities mount to the wall studs and let you choose the height.

How wide does a double-sink vanity need to be?

Plan on at least 60″ of clear wall for a comfortable double-sink layout - that gives each basin enough room and counter space between them. 72″ is roomier. Below 60″, a single sink with more counter usually works better.

How much do RTA vanities cost?

Vanities start at $119.99 per linear foot of cabinetry - a 60″ double-sink vanity is about 5 linear feet, so from roughly $600. See the cabinet cost guide for more, and we quote your exact size and finish for free.

Can the cabinet work around my existing plumbing?

Usually yes. Send the plumbing height and position with your measurements and we design the cabinet to clear or notch around the supply and drain lines. Floating vanities give the most flexibility for awkward plumbing.
