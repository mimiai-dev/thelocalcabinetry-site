Shipping footprint of a kitchen: flat-pack, assembled or imported

How much fuel does it take to get a kitchen's worth of cabinets to your door? This page estimates the carbon dioxide equivalent (CO₂e) from the truck and ship legs for a typical 10×10 kitchen, three ways. It counts transport only, not making the cabinets. The flat-pack and assembled routes start at our Houston warehouse (77020); transport to Houston is not counted. Enter your ZIP to see your own numbers.

What this does and doesn't show

  • It compares delivery of the same cabinets three ways. Flat-pack cartons pack tightly; assembled boxes take about three to four times the space on a truck, and trucks are limited by space as well as weight.
  • It does not count making the cabinets, packaging production, the trip from the factory to the Houston warehouse, or anything after delivery (your own trip to a store, assembly, disposal).
  • The imported bar describes a generic import, not any particular supplier's cabinets or ours. The EPA factor used for the ship leg is an average for all U.S. waterborne freight, not for container ships specifically, so that bar could be somewhat high. The ship distance is the straight-line minimum between the ports, which makes it somewhat low. We can't say which effect is larger.
  • Where you are matters: the closer you are to Houston, the smaller the flat-pack figure. Near the West Coast the imported route can come out below the assembled one, but on these assumptions flat-pack from Houston stays lowest.
  • These are estimates. We make no claim about total environmental impact, only about the transport numbers shown here.

How we calculate

For each route: kg CO₂e = billable short tons × miles × emission factor, added up leg by leg.
