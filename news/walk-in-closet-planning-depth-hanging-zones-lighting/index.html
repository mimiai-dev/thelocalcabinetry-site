Walk-in closet planning: depth, hanging zones and lighting

By The Local Cabinetry · October 19, 2026

In this post
  1. Start with depth
  2. Divide the closet into zones
  3. Plan the light
  4. Measure the space
  5. Where we come in

A good walk-in closet is planned in zones, not filled in order. Start with what you own, group it, and give each group the right kind of space. This guide covers the three things people ask us most: how deep a closet should be, how to split it into zones, and where light matters.

Start with depth

Closets are commonly 22 to 24 inches deep, and we plan the depth with you for your room. Hanging walls need the most depth; shelves and drawers can be shallower. If the closet is wide enough, a walk-in with units on two or three walls leaves a walking aisle in the middle, and the aisle should stay comfortable to move through with a laundry basket.

Divide the closet into zones

  • Short hanging for shirts, jackets and folded-over items: two rails one above the other.
  • Long hanging for dresses and coats: one rail with the space below it kept clear.
  • Shelves for folded clothes, bags and shoes.
  • Drawers for small items. A bank of drawers also makes a good place for a mirror or a bench.

Put what you use every day at eye level and between waist and shoulder height. Keep the top shelves for things you reach for rarely.

Plan the light

Light is easy to forget and hard to add later. Put light where you look: along the hanging rails and over the shelves, so the colors you pick in the morning are the colors you see. Plan power for it before the units go in. If your closet has no window, light matters even more.

Measure the space

Sketch the room from above, measure each wall in feet and inches, and note the doors, windows and anything on the walls. Our guide to how to measure your closet shows the steps. Then tell us what you need to store and we will help you draw the layout.
