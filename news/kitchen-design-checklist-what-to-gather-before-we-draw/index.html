Kitchen design checklist: what to gather before we draw

By The Local Cabinetry · November 25, 2026

  3. Write down appliance sizes
  4. Think through how you use the kitchen
  5. Decide a few things early
  6. Where we come in

A good kitchen design checklist has five parts: wall measurements, photos, appliance sizes, notes on how you use the room, and a few early decisions. If you have those ready, we can draw a useful 3D layout on the first pass. Without them, we spend the first round asking questions.

You do not need a perfect drawing. You need numbers you trust and a clear idea of how the room works for you. Here is what to gather.

Kitchen measurements to take first

Start with a tape measure, a pencil and paper. Draw the room from above, even if the sketch is rough. Then add numbers.

  • Each wall length, corner to corner.
  • Ceiling height.
  • Window and door width, height, and distance from the nearest corner.
  • Height from the floor to the bottom of each window.
  • Anything on the walls or ceiling that cannot move easily: vents, pipes, electrical panels, soffits, radiators.
  • Where the water supply, drain and gas line come out of the wall or floor.

Measure each wall twice. Write the numbers down as you go, in inches. Do not round. A quarter inch can matter when a run of cabinets must fit between two walls.

If your walls are not square, tell us. Old houses often have walls that lean or corners that are off. Measure a wall at the top, the middle and the bottom, and send all three numbers. We have a full walkthrough on how to measure your kitchen if you want step-by-step help.

Take photos that show the problem spots

Photos fill in what numbers miss. Take them from each corner of the room, looking back across it. Then take a few close ones.

  • Each wall, straight on, with the floor and ceiling in view.
  • Corners, especially any with a pipe, vent or bulkhead.
  • Window and door trim.
  • The ceiling line, if there is a soffit or a change in height.
  • The spot where the range hood or vent leaves the room.

Put a tape measure in a few shots. It helps us check your numbers against what we see.

Write down appliance sizes

Appliances set the size of the openings, so we need their real dimensions. Look for the spec sheet from the maker. Most list width, height and depth, and many list the room needed around the unit.

Collect these for each appliance:

  • Refrigerator: width, height, depth, and how far the doors swing open.
  • Range or cooktop: width, and whether it is gas or electric.
  • Wall oven: the cutout size.
  • Dishwasher: width and height.
  • Microwave and range hood: width, and whether the microwave sits over the range or in a cabinet.
  • Sink: type, width, and whether it mounts above or under the counter.

If you have not picked a model yet, tell us which size you plan to buy. Say so plainly, and we will leave room to adjust. If you are keeping an old appliance, measure it where it sits.

Check the path into the house too. Measure the doors and hallways a large appliance has to pass through.

Think through how you use the kitchen

The National Kitchen & Bath Association (NKBA) publishes planning guidelines that designers use. They cover things like clear floor space at work areas and doors that must not bump into each other or into appliances. Keep those ideas in mind as you think about your own habits.

Two numbers from guideline summaries are useful to know. A work aisle between counters or appliances should measure at least 42 inches across for one cook, and 48 inches if two people cook together. A walkway, where people just pass through, should be at least 36 inches. Beside the refrigerator, plan about 15 inches of counter on the handle side as a landing spot. Check these against your room. If your kitchen is tight, tell us, and we will work around it.

Beyond the numbers, answer these in a few lines:

  • Who cooks, and how many people are in the kitchen at once?
  • Do you bake, cook large meals, or mostly reheat?
  • Where do you drop groceries, mail and keys?
  • Do you want a place to sit and eat?
  • What is hard about the kitchen today? Not enough counter, no pantry, a drawer that blocks the oven?
  • Does anyone have limits on reach, bending or standing?

These answers decide where the sink, range and tall cabinets go. They matter as much as the tape measure.

Decide a few things early

Some choices change the layout, so make them before design starts.

  1. Keep or move the sink, range and refrigerator. Moving plumbing or gas costs time, and it is better to know before we draw.
  2. Door style and finish. Pick a rough direction. You can refine it later, but it helps us to know if you want a simple door or something more detailed.
  3. Drawer or door base cabinets. Drawers are easier to reach into. Say where you want them.
  4. Pantry or tall storage. Tell us how much you need.
  5. Hardware. Handles or no handles changes how doors and drawers are drawn.
  6. Lighting. If you want under-cabinet lights, say so now so we can plan for them.
  7. Wall cabinet height. Tell us if you want uppers to the ceiling or a shorter run with open space above.

Where we come in

Send us:

  • Your sketch with wall lengths, window and door positions, and fixed items marked.
  • Photos from each corner of the room.
  • Appliance models or sizes.
  • A few lines on how you use the kitchen.
  • Your early decisions, and the ones you are still unsure about.
