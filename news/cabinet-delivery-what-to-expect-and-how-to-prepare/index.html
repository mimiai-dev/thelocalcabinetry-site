Cabinet delivery: what to expect and how to prepare

By The Local Cabinetry · November 27, 2026

In this post

  3. Check the shipment before the driver leaves
  4. Open every box soon

Here is what to expect from a cabinet delivery. Ready-to-assemble cabinets arrive as flat parts in cardboard boxes, often several boxes for each cabinet. A large order may travel by freight truck on a pallet. Your job is to be ready, count the boxes, note any visible damage before the driver leaves, open everything soon, and store it indoors until assembly.

How cabinets travel to your home

Our cabinets are RTA, ready to assemble. We do not assemble or install them. That means you receive panels, doors and parts that still need to be put together.

Plan for more boxes than cabinets. This Old House notes that one base cabinet can come in three or four separate boxes. A full kitchen can fill a good part of a room.

Big orders often move as less-than-truckload freight, usually shortened to LTL. Your shipment shares a trailer with other freight. According to the carrier ArcBest, a local driver handles the last leg, typically unloads with a liftgate, and the carrier sets a delivery appointment and time window with you. A liftgate is the platform on the back of a truck that lowers heavy items to the ground. Pallets of goods can come to homes this way.

Do not assume the driver will carry anything inside. For its ABF home deliveries, ArcBest says the freight is dropped at the curb, which means the driveway or near the street. Moving it indoors is up to the customer. It also asks for someone at least 18 years old to be there to sign.

Rules differ by carrier and by service level. Read your shipping notice and ask about anything it does not cover.

On timing, here is how our orders work. Lead time is about 7 days. Shipping within the US then takes 3 to 7 days, depending on your location. For custom work we confirm the production time when the order is placed. We ship from Houston and U.S. warehouses. For a standard order, that adds up to roughly 10 to 14 days. Leave some slack before you book an installer.

Get ready before delivery day

A little prep makes the handoff calm.

  • Pick a spot. Choose an indoor room with space for all the boxes and a clear path to it.
  • Line up help. Cabinet boxes are heavy and bulky. Have a second person, and a hand truck or dolly if you can.
  • Tell the carrier about access problems. Mention a gravel driveway, stairs, a gate or tight parking ahead of time.
  • Print your order. You want the item list in your hand when the truck arrives.
  • Charge your phone. You will be taking photos.
  • Be home. Give yourself extra time around the delivery window.

Check the shipment before the driver leaves

Do this at the curb, while the driver is still there.

  1. Count. Compare the number of boxes or pallets with the delivery receipt. If the count is short, write that on the receipt.
  2. Walk around the load. Look for torn wrap, crushed corners, punctures, water stains, open flaps or boxes that were retaped.
  3. Photograph it. Take wide shots of the whole shipment and close shots of any damage, including labels. ArcBest suggests asking the driver to take photos too, with one that shows the delivery address.
  4. Write it down on the receipt. Be specific. "Two boxes crushed at one corner" is far more useful than "possible damage."
  5. Then sign, and keep a copy. If the receipt is electronic, make sure your note is entered, and take a picture of the screen.

ArcBest also asks its home customers to report damage to the local service center right away, no later than 24 hours after delivery. Another carrier may set a different limit, so check yours that same day. Contact the company you bought from as well.

Open every box soon

A clean box can still hold a damaged panel. Do not wait until assembly weekend.

This Old House advises opening all the boxes, checking the parts against the list, and photographing anything broken or missing. It says most manufacturers give about five days from delivery to report problems. Freight carriers have their own clocks. Old Dominion, for example, wants hidden damage reported within 5 business days of delivery, and wants the packaging kept until the claim is closed.

The safe habit is to inspect on delivery day and report the same day.

A good routine:

  • Work on one cabinet at a time so parts do not get mixed.
  • Open boxes without running a blade deep into the cardboard. The finished face may be right under it.
  • Check doors, drawer fronts and visible end panels in good light. Look for chips, cracks, scratches and bent corners.
  • Count hardware bags against the parts list.
  • Put the parts back in their own box and mark it "checked."
  • Keep all packaging from damaged items, even if it looks like trash.

For each problem, send the order number, the box label, a short description and clear photos.

Store the boxes until assembly

Keep the cabinets inside. This Old House recommends a climate-controlled space if you will not assemble right away, away from large temperature swings. Wood also takes on or gives off moisture until it is in balance with the air around it, as Penn State Extension explains. A damp basement, an unheated shed or an open porch is a poor place to leave wood parts. A garage that swings between hot and cold is also best avoided.

While they wait:

  • Leave parts in their boxes so faces and edges stay covered.
  • Keep boxes away from water heaters, leaks, wet paint and fresh drywall work.
  • Stack them so they cannot tip, with labels facing out.
  • Group boxes by cabinet number or by wall.
  • Keep them out of the main walking path, and away from kids and pets.

Hold off on removing old cabinets until you have checked the new ones. If a part needs to be replaced, you will be glad the kitchen still works.
