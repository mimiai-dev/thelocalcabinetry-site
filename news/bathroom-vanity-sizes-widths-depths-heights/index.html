Bathroom vanity sizes: widths, depths, heights

By The Local Cabinetry · November 18, 2026

In this post
  1. Common vanity widths
  2. Depth and height
  3. Single sink or double sink
  4. Clearances to check before ordering
  5. Where we come in
  6. Sources

Most bathroom vanity sizes fall in a narrow range. Widths run from 24 to 72 inches, depth is about 20 inches, and height is 30 to 36 inches. A single sink usually fits a vanity 30 to 48 inches wide. A double sink usually needs 60 to 72 inches. Before you order, check the clear floor space in front of the vanity and the distance from the sink center to the nearest wall.

This guide gives the common numbers and the clearances we ask buyers to check. For help measuring, see our guide on how to measure your bathroom.

Common vanity widths

Width is the number you will fix first, because the wall decides it. Common vanity widths run from 24 to 72 inches.

  • 24 to 30 inches: powder rooms and small guest baths. One sink, little counter space.
  • 36 to 48 inches: the usual single-sink range for a main bathroom.
  • 60 to 72 inches: the usual range for two sinks, or one sink with a lot of counter.

Measure the wall from corner to corner, then subtract anything that sits in the way. That means door swings, trim, a toilet beside the vanity, or a tub edge. The wall length is not always the vanity length you can use.

Depth and height

Depth. Standard depth is about 20 inches from the wall to the front of the cabinet. In a tight room, narrow vanities run 14 to 18 inches deep. Anything under 14 inches wide may need a custom order, according to a trade guide we checked.

A deeper vanity gives more counter and more storage. It also takes floor space, which matters because of the clearance rule below.

Height. There are two common heights.

  • Standard height: 30 to 34 inches, with 34 inches being an average.
  • Comfort height: about 36 inches, close to a kitchen counter.

Taller is easier on the back for most adults. Shorter is easier for kids. A guest bath or a kids' bath can sit nearer 30 inches. The ADA guidance in that same guide says the countertop should be no higher than 34 inches from the floor.

If you plan a vessel sink, remember that the bowl sits on top of the counter. A bowl 4 inches tall calls for a counter no taller than 32 inches. A 6-inch bowl calls for about 30 inches. Otherwise the rim ends up too high.

Single sink or double sink

A double sink sounds useful, but it only works when the width is there. Most guides put the floor at 60 inches for two sinks. You can squeeze two sinks into 48 inches in some cases, but there is almost no counter between them and each person gets very little elbow room.

The Bob Vila design guide, which follows NKBA recommendations, gives 30 to 36 inches between the centers of two sinks. That spacing is a good test. Two sinks at 30 inches apart, plus the space from each sink to the nearest wall, adds up fast. This is why 60 inches is the practical minimum.

Some things to weigh:

  • Two people use the room at once: a double sink helps.
  • One person uses it: a wider single-sink vanity with more counter is often the better use of the wall.
  • Plumbing: two sinks mean two drains and two supply lines. Check your wall and floor for pipes before you pick a layout.

A 72-inch single-sink vanity is also common. You get a lot of counter and storage without a second basin.

Clearances to check before ordering

These are the numbers that stop an order from becoming a problem. The figures below come from the NKBA recommendations as reported by Bob Vila. Your local building code may differ, so confirm with your local building department.

CheckRecommendedCode minimum
Clear floor space in front of the vanity30 inches21 inches
Sink center to side wall20 inches15 inches
Between two sink centers30 to 36 inchesNot stated
Space beside the toilet to a vanity or other fixture18 inches15 inches

A few notes on how to use them.

  1. Front clearance: measure from the front of the vanity, not from the wall. A 20-inch deep vanity in a bathroom 5 feet wide leaves less room than you think once the door swings in.
  2. Sink center: find where the sink will sit in the cabinet. On a vanity pushed into a corner, the sink center is not the middle of the vanity.
  3. Toilet side: if the vanity sits beside the toilet, count the space from the toilet center to the vanity edge. The same guide gives a 36 by 66 inch toilet compartment as the recommendation, and 30 by 60 inches as the code minimum.
  4. Doors and drawers: open them on paper. A door that hits the toilet or a drawer that stops at the tub edge is a common mistake.
  5. Baseboard and walls that are not square: note them. A vanity that is a half inch too wide will not go in.
